Why Does a Manufacturer Still Ask Questions When the Drawing Is Complete?

A complete technical drawing may define the part clearly and still lead to questions during quotation or manufacturing review.

These questions do not necessarily mean the drawing is incomplete. They often arise because the manufacturer still needs to determine how the specified requirements will be produced, controlled and verified under the conditions of the actual project.

Complete Drawing ≠ Complete Manufacturing Context.

The drawing defines what the part needs to be. Manufacturing questions arise when something still needs clarification before those requirements can be evaluated without assumptions that could affect quotation or production.

Why Can Questions Remain After the Drawing Is Complete?

01 · Quantity

What Quantity Is the Drawing Being Evaluated For?

The drawing defines the required part, but it does not always define the production quantity being evaluated.

Prototype, small-batch and repeat-production quantities may involve different setup economics, material purchasing, manufacturing approaches or secondary-process conditions.

This is why a manufacturer may ask for quantity even when the technical requirements are already shown on the drawing.

02 · Manufacturing Clarification

Does a Requirement Need Clarification Before Manufacturing?

A drawing may define the required dimensions, tolerances, materials and surface requirements while a particular feature or combination of requirements still raises a manufacturing question.

For example, a manufacturer may need to clarify a tolerance relationship, treatment requirement, inspection expectation or another condition that materially affects how the part will be produced or verified.

This does not automatically mean the requirement should change. The purpose is to avoid making an assumption that could affect the manufacturing judgment.

03 · Function-Critical Context

Is a Particular Requirement Especially Important to the Application?

A manufacturer does not normally need detailed information about the complete machine or assembly.

However, if a feature is critical to positioning, fit, motion, assembly, wear or another function—and that importance is not obvious from the drawing—limited application context may help identify where manufacturing or verification attention matters most.

Context is useful only when it changes how a requirement should be evaluated.

04 · Existing Project Evidence

Has Something Already Happened That the Drawing Alone Does Not Show?

Sometimes the manufacturing question comes from the current project situation rather than the drawing itself. For example:

  • another supplier has raised a manufacturing concern;
  • a drawing change has been proposed;
  • a sample passed but repeat production became unstable;
  • the current MOQ does not fit the required quantity; or
  • different suppliers responded differently to the same drawing.

In these situations, the drawing remains the technical reference. The relevant supplier, sample, inspection or production evidence helps identify what manufacturing question needs to be answered now.

When Are Additional Manufacturing Questions Reasonable?

Additional questions may be useful when:

  • the required quantity is unclear;
  • a tolerance, treatment or feature materially affects the manufacturing approach;
  • several requirements need to be controlled together;
  • a function-critical requirement is not obvious from the drawing;
  • supplier feedback has raised a specific manufacturing concern; or
  • sampling, inspection or production results create a question the drawing alone cannot answer.

This does not mean every drawing should generate a long list of questions. For a straightforward part, the drawing and required quantity may be enough to proceed.

Additional information is useful when it changes the manufacturing, quotation or verification judgment—not simply because more information is available.

What Is the Manufacturing Question?

A complete drawing answers an important question: What part is required?

Manufacturing review needs to answer another:

The Manufacturing Question What, if anything, still needs clarification to evaluate how these requirements can be produced, controlled and verified under the actual project conditions?

A manufacturing question does not automatically mean there is a problem with the drawing, nor does manufacturing difficulty automatically mean a requirement should change.

The purpose is to identify assumptions that could materially affect the manufacturing judgment before those assumptions become part of the quotation or production plan.

What Information May Be Needed Beyond the Drawing?

The answer depends on the manufacturing question being raised.

Required Quantity Quantity helps define the production conditions under which the drawing is being evaluated.
Specific Manufacturing Question or Supplier Feedback If a supplier has raised a concern, requested clarification or proposed a drawing change, the specific question or feedback helps identify what needs to be reviewed.
Relevant Sample, Inspection or Production Evidence If the question appeared during sampling or repeat production, sample results, inspection information or production feedback may help determine what has actually occurred.
Critical Application Context — When Relevant If a requirement is function-critical and its importance is not clear from the drawing, limited application or assembly context may help explain why that requirement matters.

Not every project requires all of this information.

Start with the drawing and required quantity. Add only the evidence that helps answer the specific manufacturing question.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a manufacturer asking questions mean my drawing is incomplete?

Not necessarily. A drawing can completely define the required part while questions remain about quantity, manufacturing conditions, verification or relevant project evidence.

Why does a manufacturer need quantity if the drawing is already complete?

The drawing defines the part requirements, while quantity helps define the production conditions under which those requirements are evaluated. Quantity can affect manufacturing approach, setup economics, material purchasing and secondary processes.

Should I change the drawing if a manufacturer questions a requirement?

Not automatically. A manufacturing question may identify something that needs clarification, but difficulty with one manufacturing approach does not necessarily mean the drawing requirement itself needs to change.

Do I need to explain the complete application to the manufacturer?

Usually not. Application or assembly context is useful when it helps explain a function-critical requirement or materially changes the manufacturing judgment.
